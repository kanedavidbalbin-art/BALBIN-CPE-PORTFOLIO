03 / PERSONAL JOURNEY

Growth begins
outside comfort.

My first semester at Mapúa has taught me that becoming an engineer is about more than learning technical concepts. It is also about learning how to adapt, take responsibility, and keep moving forward.

01

A new environment

Coming from Bacoor, Cavite, and spending most of my life at Statefields School Incorporated, I was used to a familiar environment and routine. Entering Mapúa meant adjusting to a new campus, a different learning system, and a faster academic pace. There were moments when I missed the comfort of high school, but I began to understand that change is part of growing.

02

Learning to manage independence

College has challenged me to take greater responsibility for my deadlines, study schedule, and learning. The quarterly system moves quickly, so I’m learning to review lessons regularly, identify topics I don’t understand, and break larger tasks into smaller steps instead of leaving everything until the last minute.

03

Commuting and perseverance

Commuting to campus and returning home exhausted has made time management especially important. I’m learning that planning my tasks, making room for rest, and being consistent can help me balance academic responsibilities with my personal life.

04

Becoming more capable, one step at a time

These experiences have shown me that choosing what is familiar can limit the opportunities we give ourselves. Computer Engineering may not have been my first choice, but I want to stay committed to the path I have chosen. Every challenge is a chance to become more resourceful, disciplined, and prepared for the future.

“Life comes at you hard, but fight harder—choosing to act is a step that opens up your future.”— A lesson from my first year
